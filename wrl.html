The Vision

Not a reskin. Built for Wheelchair Rugby League.

The aim is to make a sports game that feels specific to the real thing: chair momentum, body position, passing under pressure, hard collisions, team shape, player individuality and the controlled chaos that makes Wheelchair Rugby League brilliant to watch and play.

01 / MOVEMENT

Chair-first handling

Acceleration, momentum, steering, pushing and body position designed around the behaviour of a sports wheelchair.

02 / GAMEPLAY

Real match systems

Passing, kicking, possession, tries, tackling, tags, tipping and restarts developed as connected gameplay systems.

03 / PLAYERS

Attributes you can feel

Speed, acceleration, agility, handling, tackling and other ratings are intended to change how individual players actually behave on court.

04 / IDENTITY

Built from the community

Player personalities, chairs, kits, venues and presentation designed to give the sport and its people a genuine identity.

Devlog 001 · Chair Mechanics

Body Position Matters: Building Leaning & Agility

Working in-game

Why it matters

In WRL, the player is not fixed upright in the chair. Body position changes reach, balance, space and how difficult a shoulder tag is to access.

In-game

Lean mode gives the right stick direct control over upper-body position, turning leaning into a deliberate input rather than a cosmetic animation.

Player stats

Agility maps to maximum sideways lean, currently from roughly 6° at very low Agility to around 22° at maximum.

UE5 notes

The system is being built primarily in Blueprints and connects player data directly to functional movement rather than keeping ratings decorative.

What comes next

Lean position will feed into stability and, later, real tag accessibility based on body position, reach, angle and contact state.

Devlog 002 · Gameplay

Using Your Weight: Leaning Changes Chair Handling

Working in-game

Why it matters

Body position in a sports wheelchair is part of driving. Leaning should change what the chair can do, not simply make the character look more animated.

In-game

Forward lean during a sprint can provide a small speed gain, while leaning into the direction of a turn increases rotation and makes committed turns feel sharper.

Player stats

Because Agility controls available lean, more agile players gain a larger functional body-position range to exploit these handling effects.

UE5 notes

The current tuning reaches about 5% maximum forward-lean sprint boost and roughly 40% lean-assisted turn boost. Analogue diagonal movement was also corrected.

What comes next

The same lean direction used to gain performance will become part of the risk model for instability and tipping.

Devlog 003 · Chair Mechanics

Finding the Tipping Point

In development next

Why it matters

Fast turns, poor body position and contact all affect chair stability in the real game. A convincing WRL game needs that risk to be part of decision-making.

In-game

Speed, steering input and lean direction will combine into a stability calculation. Leaning into a turn should help; leaning away from it should make the chair less secure.

Player stats

Agility, Recovery and later contact-related attributes can give different players different margins for control and recovery instead of using one universal tipping threshold.

UE5 notes

The target is a readable transition: visible wheel lift or chair tilt first, then a recoverable danger state, then a full fall when the instability becomes too great.

What comes next

Contact will feed into instability, and a ball carrier tipping while in possession will eventually count as one way a tackle can be completed.
